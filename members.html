Jihwan Myung

Professor

Jihwan Myung is professor and principal investigator at the Laboratory of Braintime at Taipei Medical University Shuang Ho Hospital. He was born in Seoul, Korea and studied economics, physics, physiology, and life sciences in Seoul, Pohang, Seattle, and Kyoto. He investigates how various scales of time are kept in the brain, specifically in a small network clock called the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN). In his publications in 2015, he showed that the SCN network has an asymmetric coupling structure that can modulate the degree of the variance among component clocks, and use this variance to represent information of seasonal day length in addition to time of the day. These works proposed an answer to one of the oldest problems in chronobiology, known as photoperiodic encoding, and provided a new possibility that the network principle governing the seasonal timing is also responsible for the slow adaptation we experience during jet lag.

On-line profile available at Taipei Medical University Academic Hub & Google Scholar.

Lalitha Palanivelu

Postdoctoral Researcher

With academic training in biochemistry and neuroscience, Lalitha Palanivelu’s research is driven by a longstanding interest in understanding the dynamic processes that govern brain function across health and disease. During her doctoral training at Taipei Medical University, she investigated spinal neuromodulation and deep brain stimulation in autism spectrum disorder and Alzheimer’s disease models, gaining extensive experience in translational neuroscience, animal models and microsurgical techniques. Her current research explores circadian rhythms, cerebrospinal fluid dynamics, and choroid plexus biology, with the broader aim of uncovering how rhythmic and fluid-mediated mechanisms shape brain development, physiology, and neurological disorders.

Outside the laboratory, Lalitha is an avid solo backpacker, professional Indian classical dancer, gardener, painter, and diving enthusiast. These pursuits continue to enrich and inspire her intellectual curiosity regarding movement, rhythm, and exploration.

Vuong Truong

Ph.D. student

Vuong Truong is a PhD student in circadian biology interested in how biological timing emerges and is organized across scales, from cellular and molecular dynamics to system-level rhythms. His current work focuses on the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) under T-cycles, studying how environmental periodicity shapes circadian network dynamics. He takes an integrative approach, combining wetlab experiments with computational modeling and time-series analysis to better quantify and interpret rhythmic structure in biological data. In a side project, he works on embryonic heart development, developing tools to jointly analyze bioluminescence signals and heart-beat imaging to understand when rhythmic activity first emerges in cardiac tissue and how it may develop independently of brain inputs.

Lillian (Lily) Byer

Master's student

Lily earned her Bachelor's degree in neuroscience at Davidson College and worked for two years at Boston Children's Hospital as a research assistant in Dr. Maria Lehtinen's lab. Currently, she is pursuing her Master's degree in the Graduate Institute of Mind, Brain, and Consciousness at Taipei Medical University. Her previous research experiences in undergrad and postgrad in the visual system, neuroimmunology, and pathology led her to be intrigued in the circadian regulation of the choroid plexus. In the lab, she is interested in how different circadian periods affect cerebrospinal fluid production and metabolite clearance in diseases such as hydrocephalus and Alzheimer's. Outside of the lab, she enjoys dragon boat, bouldering, and exploring Taipei.
